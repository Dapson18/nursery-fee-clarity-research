Published-method comparison · 31 October 2026

How existing nursery fee tools work — and the difference we're testing

We're examining one specific question: can a parent start with an original nursery fee schedule and a proposed booking, then receive an evidence-linked calculation or an exact explanation of which essential condition is unresolved?

This is a comparison of what seven suppliers publicly describe—their inputs, processing steps and outputs. It is not a claim that any supplier lacks an undocumented feature or that our current software has outperformed their products.

The proposed workflow

Original fee schedule + intended booking → rules supported by quoted source passages → check booking conditions, period and extras → calculate a justified amount or ask a specific follow-up question.

Current status: The public demonstration uses invented rules entered by the developer; it does not automatically read real nursery documents. In unfamiliar-provider testing, just 4 of 12 numerical cases matched provisional reference totals, with eight withheld. The automated approach needs further validation.

A concrete difference — without pretending competitors fail

Fictional minimum booking example

Suppose a document states: £72 per full day and minimum booking of two full days weekly. A request for one day has a mathematical multiplication of £72, but it does not have a justified bookable price under those stated rules.

A rate-only calculation that receives just £72 × 1 lacks the minimum-booking condition. A correctly working source-first rule verifier would withhold the amount and ask whether a different booking arrangement is possible. This shows a logical difference between two specified methods, not evidence that any named competitor makes the error.

What we can and cannot conclude

  • Supported: published workflows start from different kinds of data—quotes, invoices, provider-managed bookings, provider listings or input rates—and overlap with aspects of our proposal.
  • Supported as a method proposition: enforcing source-backed tariff conditions can avoid unjustified cost figures when a simpler rate-only method lacks required conditions.
  • Not yet proved: our implementation's accuracy on new nurseries, lower user effort, superiority to competitor outcomes, commercial demand, or a defensible replication barrier.

Our research will test comprehension, parent input effort, usefulness and decision correctness. We will maintain source evidence separately from user opinions; positive feedback is not proof of calculation accuracy.
